    Mute wins, solo narrows. A material shows if nothing is soloed and it isn’t muted, or something is soloed and it is one of them and isn’t muted. Soloing a muted material shows nothing, and the lit M beside it says why.

    Rows are bottom‑of‑stack last, matching how you read a layer stack in an engine: rock at the bottom of the list is underneath. Drag the grip to reorder.

    Mute persists with the project. Solo does not — it is something you do for ten seconds, and reopening a project to find one material showing with no memory of why is the trap the node pane’s filter already avoids.

    The list is every Material node in the graph, so the panel never invents materials — it only arranges ones you made. A tick means it is in this set, and clicking toggles it, so one menu owns membership in both directions.

    That is deliberate: a delete control on every row would cost width the 260 px does not have, and would sit permanently under your cursor for an action you take rarely. Right-click on a row is the fast path when you already know which one to drop.

    Clicking a row selects its node on the canvas, so the right inspector shows that material. The two panels divide the work: the material is a node, so its colour and name are edited on the right like any node’s parameters, and the left arranges which materials are in the set and in what order. The swatch and name here identify the node; they are not a second place to edit it.

    A material can sit in several sets at once. That is what makes A/B cheap: two sets over the same materials, different order, flip the dropdown.

    A row whose Material node no longer exists is kept and marked rather than dropped silently. You deleted a node, not a set; being told is better than the set quietly getting shorter.

    It contributes nothing to the composite — no node means no selection, so there is nothing to draw. The terrain simply shows one material fewer. Remove the row when you are ready, from the right-click menu or the membership list.
